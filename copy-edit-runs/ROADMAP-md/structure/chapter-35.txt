- 6896–6907: Gas Man's Picture shows tension, not where the agent is.
  - The Gas Man Workbook's Figure 2-1 (printed p. 11) draws seven compartments as containers of one width: circuit, alveoli, arterial blood, vessel-rich group, muscle, fat and venous blood.
  - Each container fills "to a height representing partial pressure or tension in that compartment" (printed p. 11).
  - The only amounts shown are two totals in litres, Uptake and Delivered. A setting turns these into money.
  - Appendix E (printed p. 188) gives the Picture's purpose as "viewing and manipulating the state of an experiment at a given point in its evolution". It also says "the compartment schematic helps you visualize the relationships between compartment tensions".
  - Conclusion: the Picture shows where the tension is, not where the agent is. Showing where the agent is is the lesson planned-milestone item 27 was written for, and item 27 now says so.
- 6909–6920: containers drawn to scale do not work in this model.
  - A container whose width is its capacity holds an amount equal to its filled area.
  - Mapleson's electric analogue rests on the same relation, q = C·v: a condenser's charge equals its capacitance times its voltage (Mapleson WW, *J Appl Physiol* 1963;18(1):197-204, https://doi.org/10.1152/jappl.1963.18.1.197, read from the private reference corpus, equations 5 and 6).
  - This model's capacities span almost three orders of magnitude. For sevoflurane, fat holds 493 L of gas equivalent and venous blood 0.79 L, a ratio of about 620.
  - In a View 600 px wide drawn to scale, fat takes about 500 px. The circuit, alveoli, venous blood and vessel-rich group take 1 to 7 px each.
  - Isoflurane and desflurane give the same picture.
  - The figures come from `AgentUptakeSystem.for_agent` run on the shipped parameter files on 2026-10-25.
