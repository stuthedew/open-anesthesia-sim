- 8674-8684 (item 29, continued) A symbol maps to an expression, not to an identifier. Identifiers carry their unit or kind (`alveolar_ventilation_l_min`). The compartment object supplies the subscript, so F_A in code is `alveoli.<accessor>`. The mapping is the fourth column of `docs/MODEL.md` § "Symbols" (`PL-H46J`), which makes the pass finite and checkable. Beside it sits one name per modelled quantity. `core/` gives the partial-pressure-equivalent fraction eight names across four compartments, and `PL-9SH6` collapses them to one. Symbol-to-identifier was the shape guessed before the pass was measured.
- 8686-8693 Equations are both in the code and cited from it. `docs/MODEL.md` writes the differential equations, and `core/` implements their analytic solutions. The spec carries a solved form in exactly one place. A docstring that restates the solved form supplies the missing half. Rule: always cite, restate only the solved form the spec lacks, never restate what the spec states (`PL-VZL0`).
- 8695-8702 Three mechanical rules: every Code cell resolves to a real attribute; the retired accessor names never reappear; a partition-coefficient identifier names both phases in order (`PL-FZ6T`). A unit-suffix check was considered and rejected, because suffixes are already near-universal and the check would be a pure ratchet whose vocabulary needs maintaining. Judgment remains: would a reader who knows the domain guess this?
- 8704-8717 The owner supplied two references during scoping, and they settled questions the code could not. Hendrickx and De Wolf: the gas-phase cascade is F_D → F_I → F_A, with no F_C. So the middle state is named after its container, and the denominator of the F_A/F_I curve cannot be found by name in the code (`PL-3TLK`). They also record that "fraction", "concentration" and "partial pressure" are interchangeable in the gas phase, which settles the accessor name against a convention that distinguishes them. Baker and Farmery name one partition coefficient three ways in one chapter, so the ratio-naming rule is enforced mechanically (`PL-212V`).

- 8772-8790 MAC basis (project owner, 2026-09-02). In the release that adds age, `mac_percent` moves to the Mapleson MAC40 basis. - 8807-8821 The trade. Fat is pivotal for volatile agents: its tissue:gas coefficients are 34 for sevoflurane, 70 for isoflurane and 13 for desflurane. Between two patients of the same mass and different composition, fat separates them, and a rule keyed on total mass cannot express that. The direct route buys one fewer layer of unsourced derivation, because a composition estimate needs its own formula and provenance. An unsourced scalar ahead of the dominant compartment is the "plausible but incorrect clinical value" `CLAUDE.md` forbids, as surely as no scalar. Whichever rule is chosen is sourced before it ships. - 8823-8838 Cardiac output scales, as part of a coupled package (`PL-YKSM`, 2026-09-08). This decides which quantities weight scaling includes. A time constant is V_i λ_{i:b}/Q_i. Scaling the flows while volumes stay at the stored fixed litres gives τ ∝ M^{-3/4}: a heavier patient equilibrates faster and a lighter one more slowly. At 20 kg with the shipped sevoflurane coefficients, the child has 2.64 times the adult's time constants, the opposite of every textbook. Scaling volumes with the flows gives 0.76 times; scaling nothing gives 1.00. The half-measure is worse than the status quo. Compartment volumes, alveolar gas volume, alveolar ventilation and the perfusion fractions move with cardiac output and the tissue flows, or none of them moves. `PL-YKSM` carries the arithmetic and the refused alternative.

- 9076-9079 Item 34: take Blender's window management as the model. The window is divided into resizable, modular areas, each holding one view. Task-oriented layout presets can be switched, customized and saved by the learner.
- 9081-9084 Requested by the owner on 2026-09-12, naming four properties: resizable windows, modular views, task presets, and presets the user can customize and save rather than a fixed shipped set.
- 9086-9097 Why Blender: its window system is tiling, not floating. Resizable areas do not overlap and each hosts one view; the user splits and joins them from corners and borders. Areas are grouped into Workspaces, shown as tabs, each geared to a task, which the user can reorder, duplicate and delete. A layout persists when saved into the startup file. Tiling earns its place: no panel overlaps or hides another, and a covered value is a misread value, which `CLAUDE.md`'s standard treats as a safety failure. Source: Blender Manual, "Interface → Window System → Areas" and "→ Workspaces".
- 9099-9105 The vocabulary is Blender's concept (project owner, 2026-09-16: "the workspace/area concept ... is what I want to implement"). The terms were read at the source; the manual's "Areas" and "Workspaces" pages were supplied directly because the session egress proxy refused `docs.blender.org` when this was written, though the host answered a probe on 2026-09-26 (`PL-M701`). Three terms, two adopted from Blender.
- 9107-9108 An Area is a rectangle reserving screen space. It holds one thing, and areas never overlap.
- 9109-9110 A View occupies an area and has the functionality. Item 36 is the catalogue of views.
- 9111-9114 A Workspace is a set of areas holding views, geared to a task and switched as tabs. Blender puts the tabs in the Topbar, saves workspaces in the file, and lets a custom set become the defaults.
- 9116-9122 Docking is the manual's word for corner-drag operations. A corner drag can join, split or both; dragged into the middle of a second area, it replaces that area. Adjacent areas swap through the border's Area Options, and any two through Ctrl-LMB from a corner. Resizing is a border drag; modifier keys snap the border or move aligned borders together.
- 9124-9131 Two precedents from the source. Blender refuses to delete the last workspace, structurally rather than by warning, the same shape `PL-WLWY` settled for the minimum display. A workspace carries settings: Pin Scene switches back to the remembered scene. The equivalent here is a workspace that pins which run it shows, which a broken-out window needs in order to name its run.

- 9161-9198 The occlusion rule: nothing may cover a value the display is required to show while the application believes it is showing it. A floating panel and a broken-out window dragged over the main window are two ways to break it. The rule belongs to the display, not to a window type, and is stated once. Settled (`PL-WLWY`, project owner, 2026-09-15) in `docs/MODEL.md` § "Minimum displayed outputs" → "What this list requires once the layout is the reader's". The list has three parts. Unconditional: no workspace may remove it and nothing may cover it; it is stated as a test, with an invariant tier and a per-substance tier, so the inhaled agent's concentrations and MAC are today's instance, not permanent (project owner, 2026-09-16). Accounting: reachable, not simultaneously visible. Conditional: applies only when its surface is shown, so removing a chart removes no required value. The unconditional set sits outside the area system, which is the guarantee v0.6.0 builds. A second window (project owner, 2026-09-16, ratified, answering `PL-W54S`, chosen over that item's own two live readings): every top-level window carries the invariant tier and the name of its run; the per-substance tier appears once, in a main window that cannot close while another is open. The tiers are unconditional for different reasons, which carry over differently. `docs/MODEL.md` carries the argument, the two refused readings and the limit on observable occlusion. This was recorded before break-out because it decides the region's shape in v0.6.0. Later floating must comply with the rule. The division is a stepping stone, rigid while the layout apparatus is built and expected to be revisited; the occlusion rule must survive.

- 9219-9232 The layout is a nested-splitter tree (`PL-3J2P`, decided 2026-09-16 on research the owner asked for). The options were Blender's shared-vertex graph (each area names four corner points shared with neighbours) and nested splitters, decided by measurement against a threshold written first. Across all 32 workspaces Blender ships (11 defaults, 21 in five application templates), none needs an arrangement a tree cannot express. One has a four-way junction, where the tree splits one crossing border into two handles. The two behaviours a tree cannot express are opt-in: extend-drag (Shift on a border drag) and snap-merge (a separate operator on the border's right-click menu).
- 9234-9245 One supporting argument failed. A tree is strictly less expressive: five full-span splits and one ordinary join reach a five-area pinwheel that no sequence of full-span cuts can produce. The tree is chosen knowing this, on four grounds: Blender's designers never used the extra expressiveness in 32 layouts; this audience picks a workspace rather than authoring one; the limit shows as an unavailable join, not a wrong value; a planar subdivision is owned by one person forever. `docs/interface-provenance.md` carries the count, method, construction and reversal condition.
