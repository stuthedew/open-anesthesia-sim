Closes `PL-GVXP` (separate the six chart traces by more than colour, and meet contrast minima).

## The problem

The six compartment traces were separated by colour at pairwise ratios of 1.01–1.26, and circuit and vessel-rich were **both solid** — so for one of the closest pairs the redundant channel was redundant in name only. Reading one compartment against another is the whole lesson of this chart, and a reader who takes a value off the wrong curve has misread a clinical quantity.

## Line style is now the separating channel

All six differ. Vessel-rich takes a sixth pattern, `even dash` `[6, 6]` — equal mark and gap, the one rhythm no other trace has.

| Compartment | Style | Pattern (px) | Width |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Circuit | solid | — | 3 |
| Alveolar | long dash | 10, 4 | 3 |
| Mixed venous | short dash | 4, 3 | 2 |
| Vessel-rich | **even dash** | **6, 6** | 2 |
| Muscle | dotted | 2, 3 | 2 |
| Fat | dash-dot | 12, 4, 2, 4 | 2 |

Which style lands on which compartment is derived from the colour matrix rather than chosen: the pairs hardest to separate by colour get the marks easiest to separate by shape. The set's one confusable pair (2 px dots against the 4 px short dash) is spent on mixed venous against muscle, the widest-separated pair of the six. `[8, 8]` was tried first and rejected — against the alveolar trace's `[10, 4]` it differed in mark length alone, and those two sit at 1.08 under simulated deuteranopia.

## Two colours moved, and not for pairwise separation

`ALVEOLAR_COLOR` `#18A999` → `#159789`, `MUSCLE_COLOR` `#D97706` → `#D17206`. Each keeps its hue and saturation exactly and is darkened to the lightest shade clearing 3.2:1 against the panel in all four vision models. The other four already clear the floor everywhere.

**Re-picking the whole palette for luminance separation was measured and declined.** With hue and saturation held, the best achievable worst-pair across four vision models is **1.28**, reached only by driving four of six traces to near-black; and the six-trace ceiling is **1.48** once the 3:1 floor caps every trace's luminance (down from the 1.84 previously recorded, which spends a luminance range no legible trace can use). Both are less than half of SC 1.4.11's 3:1. Spending the palette's hue identity to move 1.01 to 1.28 buys nothing.

## The checker gained the measurement

`tools/contrast_check.py` now carries the Brettel 1997 dichromacy projection (two half-planes per deficiency, coefficients from libDaltonLens over the Smith and Pokorny 1975 cone fundamentals), a `TRACE_FLOOR` holding every trace to 3:1 against the panel in all four models as a **hard error**, and a `--matrix` report across all four.

That floor immediately found a shortfall no normal-vision check could see: `MUSCLE_COLOR` was 3.19:1 as displayed and **2.98:1** simulated for deuteranopia. `docs/MODEL.md`'s existing claim about the ISO 5360 agent colours under dichromacy was also wrong once measurable — 1.06 to 1.48, not the 1.1–1.4 it stated — and is corrected and pinned.

One measurement in the item's own problem statement did not reproduce: "Circuit against Vessel-rich … 1.01 under simulated dichromacy" measures 1.24–1.31. The finding it supported is untouched (two solid lines at 1.24 is nowhere near separable), but the figure was wrong and the whole matrix is now computed rather than quoted.

## `PL-W8DQ` is unblocked

Splitting `ALVEOLAR_COLOR` out of `ACCENT` answers the question that item deferred to this one: `ACCENT` is no longer a chart colour, so it can be darkened on the sliders' own terms. Its approach is updated to say so.
