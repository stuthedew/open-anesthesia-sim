## What was missing

`docs/MODEL.md` § "Assumptions" said only that *pressure is constant*. It never said what the constant is. Every concentration in this model is a fraction of one atmosphere — the symbol table, the readouts, the MAC divisor, the tolerance in percentage points, and `ControlInput.DELIVERED`'s recorded unit, which literally reads *fraction of 1 atm* — so the constant is 760 mmHg and the model is sea-level-only. A reader had to infer that from the units.

That is a statement-of-applicability gap rather than a defect, and `CLAUDE.md` treats those as safety-critical: a reader in Denver (about 630 mmHg) or Mexico City (about 585 mmHg) who sets 6% desflurane in this simulator and 6% on a real Tec 6 is not delivering the same anesthetic, and nothing in the simulator said so.

## What the document now says

**§ "Assumptions"** — the `pressure is constant` bullet becomes `ambient pressure is constant, and it is one atmosphere — 760 mmHg`, followed by two notes. The first states the identity the specification rests on: a gas-phase fraction and a partial pressure are the same quantity up to the ambient pressure, so at 760 mmHg they are interchangeable and `F_A = 0.02` is 15.2 mmHg. The second says where that assumption does the most work — the delivered-concentration control is a vaporizer dial, and at 760 mmHg a dial position means the *same* partial pressure whichever device class it belongs to. That is why the model needs no device-class parameter, why nothing here is wrong, and why a later reader should not "fix" it.

**§ "Known limitations"** — an altitude bullet, and four notes it points at:

- the two vaporizer classes and which of the three shipped agents is drawn from each — sevoflurane and isoflurane are variable bypass (Dräger Vapor 2000, Sevotec 5, Isotec 5, Penlon Sigma Delta), desflurane is the Tec 6's heated gas–vapour blender;
- their opposite responses to falling ambient pressure: the variable-bypass chamber saturates at a temperature-set vapour pressure, so output rises in volumes percent and partial pressure is approximately preserved, while the Tec 6 holds a fixed flow ratio, so output is a constant volumes percent and partial pressure falls;
- the MAC divisor's own sea-level dependence, and James and White's MAPP proposal for exactly this reason;
- the caveat that the `1/P` form is the dilute-vapour limit of the flow-splitting physics. Isoflurane's SVP is about 240 mmHg at 20 °C, which is not small against 760, so carrying the denominator through gives a partial pressure that overshoots rather than holds — about 16% high at 585 mmHg. The prose therefore says *approximately* preserved and stores no correction factor; a future implementation should derive the variable-bypass case from each agent's SVP, which is not stored today.

**§ "Delivery-limit and MAC parameters"** — one pointer. The three vaporizer maxima (8%, 5%, 18%) are drawn from both classes, which makes that list the one place both are implicitly present; the distinction matters only away from sea level.
