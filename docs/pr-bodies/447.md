## The contradiction

`docs/MODEL.md` § "Source hierarchy" said twice that tier 2 may never be the authority for a stored value. The same document records `venous_pool_volume_l` = 1.222 L as adopted from Davis and Mapleson 1981 — tier 2, because their Appendix derives the pool volumes from ICRP 1975 blood distribution rather than measuring them — on your 2026-09-07 decision (`PL-8ZJQ`). `PL-1JDD` making the tier and the adoption machine-readable is what put the two facts on one line.

You chose to fix the wording, not the practice.

## What changed

§ "Source hierarchy" gains a block, **"A lower tier may be adopted, but never silently"**, stating what a stored value whose authority is not a primary measurement owes a reader:

- **the tier on the entry itself** — the `tier` field, checked by `make check`, not a claim in prose;
- **why no tier-1 source was adopted** — on the entry, or in the file's `provenance_gap`;
- **the date and whose decision it was**, where a lower tier is adopted *in preference to* an available primary rather than for want of one. `venous_pool_volume_l` is named as the worked example.

It says in terms that this is a **higher** bar than a tier-1 citation clears, and is not a licence to prefer the convenient number: the reason has to be that no primary measurement of the quantity in the population exists or is reachable, never that finding one is work.

The three absolutes are rewritten rather than deleted, so the preference for tier 1 survives — "only tier 1 may be named as the authority" becomes "on its own strength"; the tier-2 bullet's "never the authority" becomes "adoptable only on the recorded decision below"; and the Lowe and Ernst paragraph adds that a book that collected rather than measured could still be adopted on the record.

### The Mapleson passage needed the most care, and its answer did not change

§ "Why the Mapleson values are nonetheless not adopted here" opened on that absolute as the first of three reasons. It now says adopting Mapleson would *take* a recorded decision, and that the two remaining reasons are why this one went the other way: a Mapleson divisor over a Gas Man trajectory would make each displayed MAC multiple a ratio between two parameter lineages, and ±7–10% confidence limits mean no MAC source makes the cross-agent comparison exact. Both were always the load-bearing reasons; the first was the one doing no work.
