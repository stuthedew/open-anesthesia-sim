Closes `PL-RFLN` (settle what causes desflurane's five-minute washout residual). Captures `PL-ZDWL` and `PL-03ZG`.

## What the item asked for, and what changed on the way

The brief ordered the cheap diagnostic first: re-run the sixth candidate row of `docs/MODEL.md` § "Desflurane's residual" — which rejected *residual rebreathing in the published apparatus* at an assumed `F_I/F_A` of 0.30 — at the 50 ml of corrugated Teflon the methods section sources, "of the order of a tenth of that ratio".

**That re-run would have been meaningless, and establishing why is the finding.** The 50 ml sits between the tracheal sampling port and the nonrebreathing valve; it holds alveolar gas at end-expiration and fresh gas at end-inspiration, which makes it a **series dead space**, not a mixing volume returning a fraction of every breath. In a model with one perfectly mixed alveolar compartment those are different mechanisms. Over one breath of the elimination with the fresh gas agent-free, the alveoli receive `V_T` carrying `V_D·F_A` and expel `V_T` carrying `V_T·F_A`, so the net is `−(V_T − V_D)·F_A` — exactly this model's `V̇_A·(F_I − F_A)` at `F_I = 0`, provided `V̇_A` is the true alveolar ventilation `(V_T − V_D)·f`.

So the apparatus's dead space is an **alveolar-ventilation decrement of `V_D·f` and nothing else**. The row is split rather than corrected: the assumed-ratio result stays as what it always was, and the apparatus's own volume gets a row run the right way.

## Run correctly, it fails as a common-mode mechanism

Measured against the −2.33 SD desflurane sits at with no dead space at all:

| f (/min) | V̇_A | Desflurane | Isoflurane n=8 | Worst wash-in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 3.70 | −1.89 SD | +1.59 SD | −0.42 SD |
| 8 | 3.60 | −1.73 SD | +1.82 SD | −0.63 SD |
| 10 | 3.50 | −1.56 SD | +2.05 SD | −0.85 SD |
| 12 | 3.40 | −1.38 SD | +2.30 SD | −1.08 SD |

It carries 0.44 to 0.95 SD of the 2.33 — a quarter to two fifths, never the whole — and buys that by pushing the isoflurane cohort that was inside its spread at +0.94 SD out to between +1.59 and +2.30. The wash-in comparison sets its own ceiling at 11 breaths per minute (sevoflurane's wash-in reaches −0.96 SD; one breath more takes it outside), and at that largest admissible decrement desflurane is **still 1.48 SD short with both isoflurane cohorts outside their spreads**.

It is the second candidate row — alveolar ventilation — at a sourced magnitude rather than a fitted one, and it fails the same way. `PL-73G7`'s requirement that any candidate be checked against the wash-in rows in the same pass is what sets the ceiling.

## The question this leaves, which is now the live one

Whether any decrement is owed at all is undetermined. Yasuda derived the alveolar fraction of ventilation from `F_M = f_A·F_A + f_D·F_I`, and the 1-l mixing chamber supplying `F_M` sits beyond the nonrebreathing valve — so the re-inspired 50 ml never reaches it, and their derived `f_A` is `(V_T − V_D,anat − V_D,app)/V_T`, already netting the apparatus out. A comparison run at *their* alveolar ventilation must not subtract it again. This model runs at 4.0 L/min, which `data/patients/reference_adult.json` records as a program default with no primary source, and which is neither quantity.

`PL-ZDWL` reads the study's own figure out of the methods text — it is the one thing the item's Route section asked for that was never read out, and it would convert the second candidate row from a free-parameter sweep into a sourced operating point.
