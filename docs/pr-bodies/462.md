Closes `PL-71CF` (the fresh-gas-flow control is labelled as a flowmeter is, but the model reads it as common-gas-outlet flow). The follow-up to `PL-CXYT` (what fresh gas flow counts, merged as `#459`), which put the definition in `docs/MODEL.md` and found that the one place a user actually reads did not carry it.

## Why

"Fresh gas flow" alone is the phrase on an anesthesia machine's flowmeter bank, and a flowmeter reads the carrier gas only. The model's own circuit balance forces `V̇_F` to be the whole post-vaporizer stream — carrier plus the vapour the vaporizer added. The two differ by `1/(1 − F_D)`: under a percent at ordinary dial settings, 22% at desflurane's 18% Tec 6 maximum, where flowmeters at 2 L/min leave the common gas outlet at about 2.44 L/min.

That factor lands on the circuit time constant `V_C/V̇_F` — the quantity the wash-in curve exists to show. Nothing the model computes is wrong; a user reading the slider as a flowmeter draws a wrong clinical inference from a correct number, which `CLAUDE.md` counts as a presentation-safety failure rather than a wording preference.

## The form, and the two options not taken

The control now draws **"common gas outlet"** under its name, smaller and italic — the form `_build_metric_panel` already uses for "Alveolar" over "end-tidal-equivalent" (`PL-NV9W`). Two lines at two sizes say which claim is the weaker one; a single joined line offers them as alternative names for one quantity. Using the idiom the interface already has means a reader meets one convention rather than two.

- **A longer single-line label** ("Fresh gas flow (common gas outlet)") was rejected: the panel is a quarter of the controls row, and the label would wrap where its three neighbours do not — the layout defect `_build_concentration_metrics` documents at length.
- **A tooltip** was rejected: not reliably reachable by touch or keyboard in Flet, and an explanation only some users see is weak for a safety-relevant definition.
