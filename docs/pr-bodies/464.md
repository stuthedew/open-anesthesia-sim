Closes PL-73G7 (explain desflurane's opposite-sign washout disagreement once rebreathing is removed).

`PL-W21J` gave the elimination comparison a test-only open-circuit driver, and it left one row unexplained: with the apparatus difference gone, this model reproduces sevoflurane and both isoflurane cohorts and washes desflurane out 2.33 published SD **too fast** — the only agent on that side, and the one place in this repository where a comparison against a human measurement misses in the direction of overstating recovery. This is what that residual is not, and what is left of it.

## The finding

Raising desflurane's vessel-rich coefficient does close the gap, and it costs almost nothing in the wash-in row: at a tissue:blood of 2.24 against the shipped 1.286, the elimination lands on the published 0.140 and the wash-in row stays inside its spread at +0.55 SD. The vessel-rich group is fully equilibrated after a 30-minute administration, so its capacity is nearly invisible in F_A/F_I and shows up almost entirely in the elimination. The two published rows are therefore not in conflict with each other.

What rules the change out is the tissue measurement rather than the model. Solving for the coefficient each cohort's published ratio demands:

| Agent | Demanded | Yasuda 1989 measured brain:blood | Distance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sevoflurane | 1.74 | 1.70 ± 0.09 | +0.4 SD |
| Isoflurane | 1.45 | 1.57 ± 0.10 | −1.2 SD |
| Desflurane | 2.24 | 1.29 ± 0.05 | +19 SD |

Two of the three land on the measurement. Desflurane's demand is nineteen standard deviations above its own and above what either other agent demands — it would make desflurane the *most* tissue-soluble of the three, inverting the ordering the source paper exists to report. The stored Gas Man coefficients are that paper's brain:blood values to within 3%, so there is no better value to move to. **`data/agents/desflurane.json` was not changed.**

Five further candidates were tested and each fails the same way — it moves sevoflurane and isoflurane as much as desflurane or more, and the published rows have no room for that, or it moves desflurane the wrong way: blood:gas (needs ×1.63, which is 10.9 SD above Eger 1987's measured 0.424 ± 0.024 and drops the wash-in row to −4.35 SD), alveolar ventilation, cardiac output (3 to 7 L/min moves desflurane only from −2.6 to −2.1 SD), the fast capacity this model omits (flat at 20% of every agent's pool), a non-ideal lung (shunt and V/Q dispersion both move it the wrong way), and residual rebreathing in the published apparatus.

So the cause is **not identified**, and the disagreement is recorded rather than corrected. Two candidates survive and neither is claimed: end-tidal sampling in a lung with ventilation-perfusion dispersion, and the published value itself. `PL-RFLN` carries what would separate them.
