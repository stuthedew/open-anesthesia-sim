Closes `PL-D188` (a brief written into an untriaged item is appended below `docket new`'s template rather than replacing it) and `PL-JL2M` (the same defect, filed three days earlier).

## The defect

`docket new` wrote four headings for a session to write over. A session holding the brief appended it *below* them instead — eighteen of the thirty-two items at the 2026-09-05 triage pass, fifteen of those carrying a full, careful brief under a dead stub. `_section_text` judges the first matching heading, deliberately, so the stub is the one read: an item with a two-page brief reported as having nothing under two required sections. Repairing them was most of that pass, and the line a reader saw was the opposite of the truth.

## The fix

**The template is now the one thing that is true at capture** — the title, under a `**Problem.**` of its own, and no headings to fill in. Appending is the operation sessions were already performing; it now composes, because the title is the terse problem statement the fuller sections elaborate. The failure stops being created rather than being detected after the fact.

`PL-JL2M` reached the same conclusion independently and argued against the alternative: teaching `brief_gaps` to accept any occurrence carrying text would pass a stub whose real brief was never written, which is the hole rather than the fix. The format is still stated in three places that outlived the fourth — the README, `docket triage`'s output, and `docket check` at `ready`.

**`brief_gaps` names the shape wherever it survives** — an empty required heading with a `**Problem.**` starting again below it — and *replaces* the empty-section message rather than joining it, since only one of the two is true of the item. Reporting one at a time loses nothing: delete the stub and a section the brief genuinely omits is reported on the next run, as missing.

**It fires on an `untriaged` item**, where the brief requirements deliberately do not reach. The exemption is not weakened, because the rule demands nothing capture chose to leave out — writing less can never trigger it; only writing a brief and leaving a template above it can. That puts the report in the session that wrote the brief, at its own `make docket`, instead of at a triage pass weeks later.

## Evidence the rule is exact

Measured over all 657 items in the store, it selects the six carrying the stub and nothing else. An elaborated heading such as `**Why it matters more than a normal benchmark.**` (`PL-LKCN`) opens a section of its own and leaves the plain heading above it non-empty, so it does not match — a rule counting repeated headings would have failed it. All six matches are closed items, which the brief checks do not reach, so the check starts clean and is a backstop rather than a speed bump on the normal path.
