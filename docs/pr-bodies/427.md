## The decision

Two branches stay overlaid on one time axis, with:

- **compartment on line style and colour, exactly as the single-run chart draws them** — nothing about a compartment's appearance changes on entering compare mode;
- **run on line width**;
- **at most two compartments drawn while two branches are shown.**

## Why the bullet was wrong, and not marginally

`ROADMAP.md` said "the run encoded by line style and the compartment by colour". `PL-GVXP` closed the next day having measured the opposite: `docs/MODEL.md` § "The six compartment traces" gives the trace-against-trace matrix, and the **worst pair is 1.01:1** against a 3:1 requirement. Colour is not a weak channel for the compartment here — it is not a channel at all, and the palette search that would have rescued it was run and declined.

## What the item's candidate list was missing

All three candidates were really arguments about which channel to *free*, because **the six-compartment encoding already spends every channel a single line has**: line style across six dash patterns, colour as the second cue, and width too — 3 px for circuit and alveolar against 2 px for the other four.

There is no unspent line-level channel at six compartments. That is what makes the cap load-bearing rather than a convenience: capping is what frees width, and it turns "one shared compartment selection across both runs" into a constraint the design rests on. Two compartments × two runs is four curves — which is also the comparison a learner actually makes.

## Why not just swap the two channels

Run-by-colour works arithmetically — colour carries two categories comfortably where it carries six at 1.01:1. It is rejected on **mode awareness**: colour means "compartment" in the single-run chart, and giving it a second meaning either side of a mode change puts two mappings on one channel, on the chart where a misread is a misread of a clinical value. `.claude/rules/expert-review.md` names hidden modes and context-dependent behaviour directly.

## The overlay survives, now with evidence

The bullet already recorded the decision against stacked panels because "the comparison is the whole point of the release". That instinct is supported: shared-space line graphs are the more efficient technique for comparisons over **small visual spans**, with separate-chart techniques winning as the span grows — and comparing one compartment against itself under two settings is a small-visual-span comparison by construction. Citation added to the bullet (Javed, McDonnel & Elmqvist, IEEE TVCG 2010;16(6):927–34).
