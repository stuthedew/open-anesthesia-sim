## The problem

`PL-83LS` made an `anticipated` `safety` or `science` finding not debt until the
hazard it describes exists, and `check_gate_reentries` read that off the class
alone. So the exclusion never stopped. A finding written against an unbuilt
milestone went on being passed over *after* that milestone shipped and the hazard
went live — which is the one event a rule about **when** a hazard begins exists to
notice.

## The change

One conjunct. `check_gate_reentries` exempts an item only where `anticipated` and
`status == "blocked"` hold together, matching
`subprojects/docket/src/docket/checks.py`'s safety-band exemption. The exemption
is then held only by an item that has written down what it is waiting for —
`checks.py` errors on a `blocked` item naming no `blocked-by` — and it lapses when
the item is promoted.

## What it costs, recorded rather than claimed away

The carve-out expires on the **promotion**, not on the blocker closing: nothing
rewrites `status` by itself. `bin/docket check`'s "every blocker has closed; it is
ready to promote" advisory is the mechanism, so the window is one grooming pass
instead of the indefinite one it replaces. The item's recommendation said "expires
by itself"; that was half right, and the docstring and `ROADMAP.md` now say the
accurate version.

**Resolving the blockers inside `check_gate_reentries` was considered and
refused.** It closes the window outright, at the price of a second copy of
`checks.py`'s resolution logic — item edges, milestone edges, `ships_with` — in the
tool whose job is to read the store rather than reason about it, and of two
readings of `anticipated` in one repository.
