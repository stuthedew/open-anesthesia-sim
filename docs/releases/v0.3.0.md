## Loop trial

v0.2.8's claim was not that the tooling exists but that *the development loop
is reliable enough to run two long scientific milestones through*. `PL-SZ56`
pre-registered five readouts, their t=0 values and their thresholds on
2026-09-02, before v0.3.0's first entry started, so that this section compares
against what was written then rather than against what turned out to be
flattering. Scored at the release commit; window `v0.2.8`..`v0.3.0`.

| # | Readout | t=0 | v0.3.0 | Threshold | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Product vs apparatus churn | apparatus 6.63x product | product 2.08x apparatus | apparatus <= product | Pass |
| 2 | Net queue change | +37 | +20 (57 in, 37 closed) | <= +20 | Pass, at the threshold |
| 3 | Escaped defects | not computed | 0 confirmed, 1 contested | 0 against a safety entry | Pass |
| 4 | Stranded items | 0 | 0 by the threshold, 1 in substance | 0 with no open PR and no commit in 48 h | Pass, contested |
| 5 | Digest self-reported blindness | firing every session | not firing | line stops, or `PL-K2ZK` closed | Pass |

**Readout 1 carries the most weight, and it reversed.** Across its own window
v0.2.8 moved 1,427 lines under `src/` and `tests/` against 9,463 under
`.claude/`, `tools/`, `subprojects/`, `bin/` and `Makefile` — it would have
failed this readout 6.63x over. v0.3.0 moves 4,623 product lines against 2,221
apparatus lines. `PL-C8MV` records that the partition credits apparatus tests
to product; reassigning `tests/unit/test_contrast_check.py`,
`tests/unit/test_doc_check.py` and `tests/unit/test_docket_digest_hook.py`
(483 lines) gives 4,140 against 2,704, or 1.53x. Both scorings are given here
rather than amending the readout after the outcome was known, which is the
disposition `PL-C8MV` recommends.

**Readout 2 passes at exactly its threshold, and its baseline does not
reproduce.** Intake 57, closures 37, net +20 against a ceiling of +20; the
open count moved 83 to 103, which is the same +20 by an independent route.

*Scored at the release commit, and corrected once.* An earlier scoring gave
+19, taken at `d7d5272` — the release branch's tip before its merge folded in
`#221`, `#222` and `#224`. The commit that cut the release is the one on
`main`, and at that commit the number is +20. Both figures are recorded
because re-scoring a pre-registered readout after the fact is the retrofit
this whole assessment exists to prevent, and because the correction is not
cosmetic: it moves the readout from inside the band to its edge. "Passes at
the threshold" is the honest statement; "passes with one to spare" was not.

*And the scoring convention now decides the verdict, which must be said
outright.* The window ends at the commit that cut the release, so the two
items this close-out itself files — `PL-NG3G` and `PL-P0QT` — fall outside it,
and the readout is +20 and passes. Counting them gives **+21, which fails**.
The convention is the defensible one, on the ground that an assessment is not
part of what it assesses, and it was written into this section in its first
commit (`1b34879`) when both scorings passed and nothing turned on it — it is
not a rule chosen once the answer was known, and any reader may check that
commit. But it is the only place in this trial where the choice of convention
changes a pass into a fail, and a reader who prefers the other convention
should have the number rather than have to derive it. On that reading the loop
took in marginally more work than the band allows for a debt-clearing release,
by one item, in a release whose own close-out filed two.

*The tag placement follows from this and is not free.* If the tag is pushed on
the commit that cut the release, the window and the tag agree. If it is pushed
on this close-out's merge instead, the tagged tree contains two items the
scored window excludes, and the readout as computed from the tag is +21. The
former is the coherent placement.

The pre-registered confound stands and is not waived: v0.2.8's +37 included a
one-off repository-wide review that filed 65 items on 2026-08-30, so a lower
number was partly expected and this is not claimed as an improvement. A second
confound was found while scoring and is filed as `PL-40PL`: readout 2 names no
command, unlike readout 1, and its t=0 figures of 141 in and 104 closed
reproduce under no method tried — the `added:`/`closed:` field dates give
138/102 and a store delta between the two tags gives 94/78. The v0.3.0 answer
is stable across all three methods (+15 to +20, inside the band each time), so
this changes no verdict; it bounds how strongly the t=0 comparison may be
stated. The `safety`/`science` subset was 5 filed and 2 still open, against 9
filed and 5 open at t=0.

**Readout 3 passes, and the one contested hit is recorded rather than
dropped.** Twenty-one `defect`-classed items were filed in the window. Under
the causal rule as amended on 2026-09-02, none is caused by `PL-026`,
`PL-0MLQ` or `PL-NV9W`. The contested case is `PL-GW37`, which names `PL-026`:
clause 1 does not fire, because its brief attributes the fault to
`_merges_naming`'s recency rule and to `CLAUDE.md`'s singular-id wording, both
older than `PL-026`; clause 2 fires on a literal reading, because the defect
reproduces at `#204` and not at its parent — `#204` created the first rider
closure. It is scored not-an-escape here, on the ground that `#204`
instantiated an older fault rather than shipping wrong work. That call is
stated rather than buried because readout 3's amendment was itself made after
seven of the nine open entries had closed, and a reader may re-score from the
ids given.

**Readout 4 passes on its threshold and is contested on its substance.**
`bin/docket stranded` reports four items existing only on a branch. Three
(`PL-CP74`, `PL-D4MZ`, `PL-YHD3`) sit on a branch committed to minutes before
this reading — a live session, which the amended threshold excludes expressly
and correctly.

The fourth, `PL-Q8QX`, is the contested one and is recorded here rather than
counted silently. Its branch carried a commit 37 minutes old, so the 48-hour
clause excludes it and the readout scores 0. In substance it is stranded: the
branch had already merged as `#223` and been deleted from the remote, and the
item never reached `main`. It was not dropped by a decision. The merge commit
that brought `main` into the release branch deleted the file as part of its
conflict resolution — no commit in that history deletes it, the merge's own
tree does — so a captured item disappeared with nothing recording that it had.
The loss was harmless only by luck: `PL-Q8QX` was a third filing of the defect
`PL-2XTF` carries, and `PL-9GCV` was the second, explicitly dropped as a
duplicate in `#224` with its reason written down. That is the contrast worth
keeping — one duplicate closed by a decision that is legible afterwards, one
removed by a merge resolution that is not. Filed as `PL-P0QT`.

This is an instrument gap rather than bad luck in the reading. The 48-hour
clause treats a recent commit as evidence of a live session, and here the
freshest possible commit sat on the deadest possible branch: merged, deleted,
and never to be merged again. Whether the branch still exists on the remote is
the check the threshold is missing, and it is decidable rather than a judgment.

`PL-21GS` records the other confound: every reading up to and including t=0 was
taken in a shallow container, where `stranded` is bounded by the refs the
checkout holds. Landing `PL-K2ZK` moved that from 6 refs to 7 and surfaced
`PL-8DJ7` immediately; this reading saw 9. The earlier zeros are therefore
`0 of what could be seen`, and the post-deepening readings are the stronger
ones; five identical zeros are not five identical measurements.

**Readout 5 passes on both limbs.** The digest's "N refs could not be compared
with `origin/main`" line does not fire, and `PL-K2ZK` closed in v0.2.12 via
`#214` with its reason recorded.

**Two confounds pre-registered at t=0, both still standing.** `PL-L9JS` (eight
open items carry a `verify:` command that passes without their work) is itself
a Gate 0 entry, so the trial repaired one of its own instruments mid-run, and
verify-command reliability at close-out is not measuring what it measured at
the start. And `bin/docket check` reported four grooming advisories at t=0,
one of them that `PL-0MLQ` — the entry `next` offered first — carried a
`verify:` command selecting no test. The instruments were partly miscalibrated
at t=0 and this section does not read their output as clean.

**Did the claim hold?** Yes, on the evidence available, and with the
confidence spread unevenly across the five. The loop closed 21 of 21 frozen
gate entries across the v0.2.6 to v0.3.0 series, product work outweighed
apparatus work for the first time in the project's recorded history, intake
stayed inside its band, nothing escaped the gates onto a safety-classed
entry, and the two instruments that were declining to answer at t=0 now
answer. Readouts 1 and 5 are strong and would survive any reasonable
re-scoring. The other three would not, and the verdict should be read knowing
that. Readout 2 lands exactly on its threshold, against a baseline that will
not reproduce, and fails under the alternative convention for what belongs in
the window. Readout 3 passes under a rule amended after most of the entries
had closed, and has one hit that a different reading would count. Readout 4
scores zero on its threshold while one item was in substance stranded — lost
by a merge resolution, and harmless only because a duplicate carried the same
finding. Three of the five turn on a judgment that is written down here rather
than on a number that speaks for itself. The loop is reliable
enough to run v0.4.0 and v0.5.0 through, which is what the claim asserted.

**What to change before v0.4.0.** The trial's own instruments needed repair
three times while it ran — `PL-C8MV`, `PL-21GS` and `PL-40PL` all came out of
measuring rather than out of the work — which is the ordinary cost of a first
run and the argument for not scoring the second one by hand. `PL-SZ56`
deliberately did not build `bin/docket vitals`, on the ground that the trial
was the evidence for whether the numbers were worth having rather than a
prediction to act on. They were. Readouts 1, 4 and 5 are already one command
each; readout 2 needs its counting rule written down, which `PL-40PL` has to
answer regardless; readout 3 needs the causal rule frozen before v0.4.0 opens
rather than amended while it runs. Gates 1, 2 and 3 each want these same five
readouts, so the rule is settled once and read three times.
